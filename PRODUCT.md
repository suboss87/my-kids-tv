# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Two users share one screen. **Children aged 4 to 13** use it from the sofa, three metres away, with a TV remote: arrows,
OK and Back. Some can't read yet. They want to find something good to watch quickly. **Their parents** set it up once,
from a computer with an AI assistant, and want to trust what their child sees: age-right, worth the time, no ads, no
tracking. Parents come back only to change the age, the PIN or the apps.

## Product Purpose

My Kids TV replaces the TV's home screen. It shows three titles at a time, from a catalog vetted for the child's age,
and only titles on the family's own streaming apps, each with one honest reason to watch it. Success: the child picks
something within seconds, the parent never has to wonder what's on screen, and the TV stops being a slot machine.

## Brand Personality

**Calm, curious, kind.** The feeling of looking up at a clear evening sky: quiet, beautiful, a little magical. It
invites curiosity rather than grabbing attention. It speaks to children as capable people, never in baby talk, and to
parents with plain honesty.

References, for specific qualities:
- **Apple TV and visionOS**: big artwork as the hero, frosted glass that means depth (not decoration), unhurried focus
  motion, restraint.
- **Disney+ and Pixar**: warmth and character carried by the artwork itself; a cinematic, story-first feel.
- **Netflix Kids**: very few choices, big friendly targets, navigation a six-year-old can't get lost in.

## Anti-references

- **The stock TV home screen** (the Haier and Google TV launchers): rows of promos, banners, autoplay, sponsored tiles.
- **Babyish cartoon UI**: primary-color blobs, mascots everywhere, bubble fonts. Older children feel talked down to.
- **Dark grown-up streaming UI**: moody black, adult key art, binge cues ("Continue watching", countdowns to the next
  episode).
- **Generic AI/SaaS look**: gray cards, purple gradients, glass on everything, stats and badges for their own sake.

## Design Principles

1. **The artwork is the hero.** The interface frames the films and shows; chrome stays quiet and gets out of the way.
2. **Three good choices beat thirty.** Fewer, better options; nothing that encourages endless scrolling or autoplay.
3. **Calm by default, delightful in the details.** Motion is slow and soft; delight lives in small moments (the focus
   lift, the greeting, the evening sky), never in noise.
4. **Every pixel tells the truth.** A badge means something (Jev's judgment, a person's review); nothing is promoted
   because someone paid.
5. **Readable from the sofa by a six-year-old.** Big type, unmistakable focus, meaning carried by pictures and color as
   well as words.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- 10-foot UI: body text at least 28 px at 1080p, titles larger; focus always visible and unambiguous from three metres.
- Contrast WCAG AA or better for all text over artwork (scrims or frosted plates behind text, never text straight on a
  busy image).
- Meaning never by color alone: badges carry a word as well as a color dot.
- Reduced motion respected (the TV's animation scale and prefers-reduced-motion): crossfades instead of movement.
- Works for pre-readers: the picture and the app logo identify every choice.
- TalkBack: every reachable item is spoken.
- 25 languages; layouts must hold longer German and Russian strings.
